Many companies offer leave benefits that allow employees to take time off from work for various reasons. Leave benefits – whether paid, unpaid or partially paid – are generally an agreement between the employer and employee, or employees representative (such as a union).
## Family and Medical Leave Act
The [Family and Medical Leave Act](/agencies/whd/fmla) provides certain employees with up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year. It also requires that their group health benefits be maintained during the leave.
## Fair Labor Standards Act
While certain types of leave are required by law, other types are voluntary incentives provided by employers. There is a common misconception that Department of Labor regulates leave benefits through the [Fair Labor Standards Act](/agencies/whd/flsa). But, the FLSA only covers certain types of leave.
In fact, there are a number of employment practices which FLSA does not regulate. For example, it does not require:
* Vacation, holiday, severance, or sick pay
* Meal or rest periods, holidays off, or vacations
* Premium pay for weekend or holiday work
* Pay raises or fringe benefits
* Discharge notice, reason for discharge, or immediate payment of final wages to terminated employees
## Statistics
The [Employee Benefits Survey](https://www.bls.gov/ncs/ebs/) of the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/) covers the incidence and characteristics of employee benefits.
